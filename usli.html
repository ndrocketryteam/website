The Competition at a Glance

The NASA Challenge

Administered annually by the NASA Office of STEM Engagement, the University Student Launch Initiative (USLI) challenges elite collegiate teams to design and construct a rocket that meets rigorous, predetermined criteria.

The objective is straightforward but incredibly demanding: design a launch vehicle that safely ascends to an exact target apogee between 4,000 and 6,000 feet, safely descends via parachutes, and executes a complex scientific payload. At the start of the academic year, the team must formally apply, outlining our approach to the competition's major challenges, strict deadlines, and rigorous safety precautions.

The Fighting Irish in Huntsville

Championship Legacy

Since its founding in the 2011-2012 academic year, Notre Dame Rocketry has been a relentless competitor on the national stage. Every year, the Fighting Irish design and document the construction of a flight-ready launch vehicle, launching several sub-scale and full-scale prototypes to qualify for the competition launch hosted by the NASA Marshall Space Flight Center in Huntsville, Alabama.

Following years of consistent growth, NDRT captured the **2024 USLI Overall National Championship**. The University Student Launch Initiative provides a unique opportunity for our students to learn both advanced aerospace concepts and the critical life skills that make them successful professional engineers.

The NASA Milestone Track

Once accepted into the annual competition, the team must keep NASA updated on their progress through major reports and their associated presentations, running parallel to real-world aerospace contracts.

01. PDR

Preliminary Design

Establishing foundational baseline parameters, dynamic modeling constraints, and baseline payload requirements.

02. CDR

Critical Design

Finalizing engineering schematics, sub-scale fluid dynamic reports, and clearing designs for manufacturing.

03. FRR

Flight Readiness

Validating vehicle physical fabrication, structural test logs, and passing full-scale qualifying test flights.

04. ADDENDUM

Final Adjustments

Submitting any late-stage refinements, payload integrations, and corrective action reports derived from the full-scale flight data.

05. PLAR

Post-Launch Review

Compiling downstream black-box engine analysis, telemetry deviation parameters, and flight recovery performance.
